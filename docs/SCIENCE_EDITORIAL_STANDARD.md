# ONEIROX Science Editorial Standard

Status: ACTIVE — 2026-09-23

## Purpose

Oneirox is a sleep-neurobiology and dream-interpretation product. Its differentiator is not sounding scientific; it is making the boundary between evidence and interpretation visible.

## Evidence labels

### ESTABLISHED
Use when the claim is directly supported by sleep physiology or a robust replicated finding.

Language: "is", "has been measured", "is associated with" only when the association is directly supported.

### SUPPORTED HYPOTHESIS
Use when a mechanism or interpretation has empirical support but meaningful uncertainty remains.

Language: "may", "is consistent with", "one supported possibility", "has some empirical support".

### SPECULATIVE / ONEIROX INTERPRETATION
Use for scene-level meaning, symbolic mapping, and any one-dream-to-one-mechanism inference.

Language: "working interpretation", "hypothesis", "could fit". Never present it as a diagnosis, neural readout, or hidden message proven by science.

## Forbidden shortcuts

- "The amygdala was rehearsing X" from one dream report.
- "The body proves what the dream was processing."
- "REM means the brain was consolidating this exact memory."
- "Dreams are diagnostic."
- "PGO waves paint this exact dream image."
- "REM atonia is why your dream legs would not move" unless clearly labeled as a hypothesis.
- Fixed mappings such as blood = higher arousal, animal = primal circuit, painless teeth = lower bruxism.
- Lunar date presented as an individual sleep-stage or REM-density measurement.

## Required structure for indexable neuroscience/dream pages

1. What is established.
2. What is plausible / supported.
3. What is not known.
4. Oneirox working interpretation, explicitly labeled.
5. Safety boundary when symptoms could be clinical.
6. Sources when the page makes specific neuroscience claims.

## Product rule

A shorter page with honest uncertainty is better than a longer page padded with confident mechanism language.

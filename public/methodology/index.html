Origin

The gap between what people say and what the body registers

Standard dream interpretation starts with the image. My method starts by recording the felt quality, the waking body state, the timing, and the life context — because those are observations we can keep separate from the interpretation we build afterward.

That distinction matters. Current dream neuroscience can describe sleep states, neural correlates, memory and emotion-related processes, and some ways waking experience enters dreams. It cannot take one image — falling, teeth, a snake — and identify one hidden neural message with certainty.

So the Oneirox rule is stricter than a symbol dictionary: physiology first, evidence level second, interpretation third. A mechanism can be established while the meaning of one dream remains a hypothesis.

“Start with observations. Separate what the science establishes from what the dream invites you to interpret.”
— Vigen G.R., founding principle

Core principles

Five principles I will not compromise on

These are not marketing bullets. They are the rules I use when I read a dream — and the rules encoded in Lab Search ranking and in every SIGNAL · BODY · MORNING page. Each one cost me a book, sometimes several.

  1. 01

    The emotional signature comes before the symbol table.

    Dream research does not show that a dream image has a fixed biological meaning. I start with felt quality and waking context because emotion and salience are measurable parts of sleep mentation, while symbolic decoding remains interpretive.

    Siclari et al. 2017 · Bloxham 2024

  2. 02

    Waking body signals are context, not proof.

    Chest pressure, jaw tension, temperature, immobility, or a jolt can be useful observations because sleep physiology can cross the wake boundary. They can constrain an interpretation, but they do not prove what a dream meant. Persistent or concerning symptoms belong in clinical care, not in a dream decoder.

    Sleep physiology + interoceptive context · dream-specific inference remains a hypothesis

  3. 03

    Mechanism and life context must stay separate.

    Sleep stage, arousal, memory reactivation, and autonomic state can shape dream experience. Waking concerns can also be incorporated into dreams. I use both layers, but I do not claim that a specific image uniquely identifies a specific neural mechanism.

    Siclari et al. 2017 · Salvesen et al. 2024

  4. 04

    Timing can be informative, not diagnostic.

    Dreams often incorporate recent or salient waking experience, but there is no validated rule that a dream appearing before a life event diagnoses that event. Timing is context that may raise or lower a hypothesis; it is not a biomarker.

    Dream-incorporation literature · effects vary across methods and samples

  5. 05

    A dream is not prophecy — and it is not a neural readout.

    Current neuroscience does not support fixed dream dictionaries or a method that can infer one hidden message from one dream. Oneirox returns a working explanation: what is established, what is plausible, and what remains speculative.

    Siclari et al. 2017 · Bloxham 2024 · Valli & Revonsuo 2009

Reading sequence

How I actually read a dream — step by step

When I sit with a dream report — or when you run Lab Search — this is the sequence. The order is practical, not sacred: observations first, plausible mechanisms second, interpretation last.

  1. 1

    The Feeling

    What is the felt quality of the dream, and what did the body feel like on waking? Record these as observations. They can be informative without being proof of what the dream meant.

  2. 2

    The Timing

    What waking-life material was recent, salient, or emotionally loaded? Dream content often incorporates waking experience, but timing is context — not a diagnosis of what the dream “really” means.

  3. 3

    The Neuroscience

    Which sleep mechanisms could plausibly contribute — REM or NREM mentation, arousal, memory reactivation, motor inhibition, sensory or interoceptive input? Then label what is established and what is only a hypothesis.

  4. 4

    The Specificity

    What distinguishes this version of the dream from other versions of the same image? Two people dream of falling. Two people dream of the same ex. The mechanism may differ. The somatic profile may differ. The life context certainly differs. Specificity is where symbol tables fail — they treat every snake as the same snake. I do not.

  5. 5

    The Working Interpretation

    State the narrowest explanation consistent with the observations and the evidence. Label what is established, supported, or speculative. Precision means refusing certainty we do not have.

Lab readout

What you receive: SIGNAL · BODY · MORNING

The reading sequence above collapses into three sections on every mechanism page. I designed this format for the person awake at 3am — not for a journal, not for a textbook. Each section has one job.

Signal

The working hypothesis in one sentence — concise enough to test against your experience, but never presented as a direct neural readout.

Body

The physiology or theory that could plausibly contribute: sleep stage, arousal, atonia, memory, interoception, threat simulation. Each claim carries an evidence level.

Morning

A precise question for reflection based on the observations above. It is not a message proven to have been “sent” by the nervous system.

Five years · still counting

How fifty books became a method

This methodology did not arrive fully formed. It accumulated — year by year, book by book, reading by reading. The timeline below is honest about that progression. I am on year five and I am not done.

  1. 01

    Foundation — Walker, Cartwright, LeDoux

    Dreaming is not random, but neither dream function nor dream meaning has one settled neural explanation. That uncertainty became part of the method.

  2. 02

    The body layer — Damasio, Sapolsky, Porges

    Body-state observations can add useful context, but they are not a decoder key. The method began separating physiology from interpretation.

  3. 03

    Depth — Jung, van der Kolk, Levine

    Recurring dreams, nightmares, and trauma-related sleep showed why clinical phenomena and symbolic interpretation must not be collapsed into one claim.

  4. 04

    Building Oneirox

    Cluster architecture. Every dream type connected to a psychological root. Research becoming structure.

  5. 05

    Instruments · Lab Search

    Sensory Dream Mapper live. Lab Search live. Depth over thin long-tail: 26 dream pillars, 400+ somatic profiles, unique mechanism keys — not a farm of near-duplicate URLs.

What changed me

Books that built this methodology

Fifty books is not a vanity metric. It is the actual count of what I read to understand why existing dream tools failed — and what a honest alternative would require. Below are the ones that changed the method most. The full shelf is larger. The research continues.

Why We Sleep

Matthew Walker

A useful synthesis of sleep, memory, and emotion. Later evidence supports important REM–affect links but does not justify treating dream content as a direct “overnight therapy” readout.

The Twenty-Four Hour Mind

Rosalind Cartwright

Longitudinal dimension — dream content tracks life transitions. Undergirds everything about dream timing in this methodology.

The Feeling of What Happens

Antonio Damasio

Body-state and feeling models influenced Oneirox’s attention to interoception. They do not show that waking body residue decodes the meaning of a dream.

The Emotional Brain

Joseph LeDoux

Threat processing can proceed without full conscious appraisal. Applying that finding to the meaning of one dream remains an inference, not a measurement.

Behave

Robert Sapolsky

Social and physical threats recruit overlapping stress and salience systems, but not identical circuitry. This is context for interpretation, not a dream code.

The Body Keeps the Score

Bessel van der Kolk

Somatic dimension of trauma — recurring dreams and nightmares that return long after the original event. When the body remembers what the mind filed away.

The Polyvagal Theory

Stephen Porges

Polyvagal theory influenced how I thought about body-state and social context, but important claims remain debated. Oneirox does not treat it as settled dream neuroscience.

The Dreaming Brain

Allan Hobson

Hobson’s brain-state and activation-synthesis work helped replace fixed symbolic messages with mechanistic questions. It does not establish dreams as diagnostic assessments.

I am still reading. If you are a researcher and you think I have missed something essential — I want to hear it. This methodology is alive.

Evidence standard

Three labels every Oneirox claim must survive

Established: directly supported sleep physiology or a robust replicated finding. Supported hypothesis: plausible and empirically informed, but uncertain. Speculative: a Oneirox interpretation or research theory that must never be written as a biological fact.

Core references for this standard: Siclari et al. 2017 on neural correlates of dreaming; Bloxham 2024 on dreaming and memory consolidation; Pesonen et al. 2024 on REM disruption and affect; and Valli & Revonsuo 2009 on threat-simulation evidence and limitations.

Chronobiology

Lunar sleep effects: interesting, inconsistent, not a decoder.

Cajochen et al. reported lower NREM delta power, slightly longer sleep-onset latency, and shorter total sleep around full moon in a retrospective laboratory analysis. Later studies produced mixed or null results. The Dream Phase Calculator is therefore exploratory context only — it cannot infer an individual night’s physiology from lunar date.

Honest limits

What this methodology cannot do

I will not pretend this method is everything. Precision requires limits. Here is what I refuse to claim — because claiming it would make this page dishonest and the readings less trustworthy.

  • Diagnose psychiatric conditions, sleep disorders, or neurological illness
  • Predict the future, lottery numbers, or outcomes you have not yet chosen
  • Replace therapy, crisis intervention, or medical care when you need it
  • Guarantee that a dream has one hidden message or that a Oneirox interpretation is biologically proven
  • Remain static — I update Lab Search, the mechanism library, and this page as the research evolves

What it can do is organize the evidence, surface plausible mechanisms, and give you a disciplined question to test against your own experience. The final interpretation remains a hypothesis.

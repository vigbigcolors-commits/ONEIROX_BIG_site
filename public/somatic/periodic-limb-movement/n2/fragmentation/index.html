Periodic limb movements during sleep: PLMS, repeated leg movements, and PLMD

PLMS are repetitive stereotyped limb movements measured during sleep. Their clinical significance varies, and one remembered kick does not diagnose PLMS or PLMD.

ESTABLISHED

Periodic limb movements during sleep are repetitive, stereotyped sleep-related limb movements that can be quantified during polysomnography.

SUPPORTED HYPOTHESIS

PLMS may coexist with restless legs syndrome and several other sleep, neurological, medical, or medication-related conditions. Their clinical relevance varies substantially between individuals.

What this cannot tell you

A remembered kick, bed-partner report, or isolated leg movement cannot establish the periodicity, movement index, associated arousals, or diagnosis of periodic limb movement disorder.

What you can actually observe

  • Whether movements appear repetitive and stereotyped rather than isolated
  • Whether a bed partner notices recurring leg or foot movements
  • Whether movements appear to disrupt sleep
  • Whether restless-leg sensations occur while awake before sleep
  • Whether there is meaningful daytime sleepiness or fatigue

Safety boundary

Persistent repetitive movements accompanied by insomnia, daytime impairment, significant restless-leg symptoms, or another suspected sleep disorder should be assessed clinically. PLMS and PLMD are not interchangeable terms.

Exploding head syndrome: a sudden sound sensation at the sleep–wake boundary

Exploding head syndrome involves a sudden perceived bang or explosive sound around a sleep transition. Its underlying mechanism remains uncertain.

ESTABLISHED

Exploding head syndrome describes brief episodes in which a person perceives a sudden loud bang, explosion, or similar sensory event around a sleep–wake transition without a matching external sound.

SUPPORTED HYPOTHESIS

Several neurological and sleep-transition mechanisms have been proposed, but current evidence does not establish one specific mechanism for all episodes.

What this cannot tell you

The experience does not demonstrate a glutamate surge, a specific EEG band, seizure activity, or a particular psychological meaning. The underlying pathophysiology remains incompletely understood.

What you can actually observe

  • What the perceived sound or sensory burst was like
  • Whether there was any matching external sound
  • Whether the event occurred while falling asleep or waking
  • Whether pain, weakness, confusion, or other neurological symptoms occurred
  • How often the episodes recur

Safety boundary

Typical isolated episodes are usually brief and benign. A new severe headache, persistent pain, loss of consciousness, focal neurological symptoms, or seizure-like events require medical assessment rather than interpretation as exploding head syndrome.

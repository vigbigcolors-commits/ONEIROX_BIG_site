Confusional arousal: incomplete awakening from NREM sleep

Confusional arousals are disorders of arousal involving incomplete awakening from NREM sleep with confusion or disoriented behavior.

ESTABLISHED

Confusional arousal belongs to the NREM disorders of arousal, in which elements of sleep and wakefulness coexist during an incomplete awakening. Episodes often arise from slow-wave sleep, although NREM stage distribution is not absolute.

SUPPORTED HYPOTHESIS

Sleep disruption, sleep deprivation, and other factors that increase or destabilize arousals can contribute to disorders of arousal in susceptible people.

What this cannot tell you

A report of confusion, mumbling, or clumsy movement cannot reconstruct an individual's EEG, identify a specific transmitter change, or distinguish every parasomnia from seizures or other disorders.

What you can actually observe

  • Approximate duration of confusion after the apparent awakening
  • Whether speech or behavior was disorganized
  • How much of the event was remembered afterward
  • Whether complex behaviors such as walking occurred
  • Whether the event created a risk of injury

Safety boundary

Frequent episodes, dangerous behavior, injury, new adult-onset events, or events that could represent seizures or another sleep disorder warrant clinical evaluation.

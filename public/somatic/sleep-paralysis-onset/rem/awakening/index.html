Sleep paralysis at awakening: what REM atonia can explain

Sleep paralysis can occur when awareness returns while REM-related muscle atonia briefly persists. Learn what the experience can and cannot tell you.

ESTABLISHED

Sleep paralysis is a dissociated sleep–wake state in which awareness can return while REM-related skeletal-muscle atonia persists briefly into wakefulness.

SUPPORTED HYPOTHESIS

Irregular sleep schedules, sleep loss, and disrupted sleep can increase susceptibility in some people. Hallucination-like experiences, sensed presence, fear, and chest-pressure sensations may accompany an episode.

What this cannot tell you

A single episode does not reveal the meaning of a dream, identify a specific psychological cause, or by itself diagnose narcolepsy, REM sleep behavior disorder, or another neurological disorder.

What you can actually observe

  • Whether voluntary movement was temporarily impossible
  • Approximate duration of immobility
  • Whether unusual visual, auditory, or sensed-presence experiences occurred
  • Whether breathing felt difficult even though respiration continued
  • Whether the episode occurred while falling asleep or waking

Related physiology

  • REM atonia

Safety boundary

Occasional isolated sleep paralysis is often benign. Recurrent distressing episodes, marked daytime sleepiness, cataplexy-like symptoms, unusual injuries, or other persistent sleep symptoms warrant clinical assessment.

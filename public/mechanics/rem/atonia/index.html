System 02 · motor hardware

REM atonia lock

During REM sleep, brainstem circuits strongly inhibit skeletal motor output. Glycinergic and GABAergic inhibition contribute to this atonia, which helps prevent most dream-related movement from being acted out.

What REM atonia is

REM atonia is a well-established motor feature of REM sleep. Brainstem and medullary circuits reduce spinal motoneuron activity through inhibitory pathways, including glycine and GABA. It is protective physiology, not a symbolic “paralysis meaning.”

Sleep paralysis is a state transition, not a dream code

When awareness returns before REM-associated motor inhibition has fully released, a person may experience brief sleep paralysis. Hallucinated presence, chest pressure, or fear can accompany the state, but those experiences are not proof of an external presence or a specific psychological message.

Movement during dreams needs a different standard

Small phasic twitches can occur in normal REM. Repeated dream enactment — punching, kicking, leaving the bed — is different and can require clinical assessment. Oneirox should not collapse normal twitches, sleep paralysis, and REM sleep behavior disorder into one continuum.

What to record

Could you move immediately on waking? How long did immobility last? Was there dream enactment or only a brief twitch? Those observations help describe the sleep event. They do not determine the meaning of the dream scene.

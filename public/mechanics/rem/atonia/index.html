System 02 · motor physiology

REM atonia and motor inhibition

During REM sleep, brainstem circuits strongly inhibit skeletal motor output. Glycinergic and GABAergic inhibition contribute to this atonia, which helps prevent most dream-related movement from being acted out.

What REM atonia is

REM atonia is a well-established motor feature of REM sleep. Brainstem and medullary circuits reduce spinal motoneuron activity through inhibitory pathways, including glycinergic and GABAergic mechanisms. This is normal sleep physiology, not a symbolic meaning attached to paralysis.

Sleep paralysis is a state transition

Sleep paralysis can occur when awareness returns while REM-related skeletal-motor inhibition persists briefly into wakefulness. Fear, sensed presence, hallucination-like experiences, or chest-pressure sensations may accompany an episode, but those experiences do not identify an external cause or a specific psychological message.

RSWA and RBD require a different standard

Small phasic twitches can occur during normal REM. REM sleep without atonia is an objective polysomnographic and EMG finding. REM sleep behavior disorder requires clinical assessment and objective sleep evidence; a dream report or isolated movement cannot establish either condition.

What to record

Useful observations include whether voluntary movement was possible, approximate duration, whether movements were small or complex, whether vocalization occurred, and whether anyone was injured. These observations describe the event without pretending to measure atonia from memory.

ObservationWhat it can and cannot show
Brief inability to move while wakingConsistent with sleep paralysis when the clinical context fits; the experience itself does not quantify REM atonia.
Small isolated twitchSmall twitches can occur during sleep; a remembered twitch does not diagnose RSWA or RBD.
Repeated punching, kicking, grabbing, or leaving the bedDream-enactment behavior warrants a different clinical standard, especially when recurrent or associated with injury.

Record motor observations

Record immobility, twitching, vocalization, and timing as observations. These details can describe the event but do not diagnose a REM disorder or decode dream content.

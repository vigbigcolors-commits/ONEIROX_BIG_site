REM dream mechanics

Below is the physiology we can defend: REM is an activated sleep state with marked motor inhibition, phasic sensory/autonomic events, and changing duration across the night. Dreaming is common in REM but also occurs in NREM, so no single REM mechanism can decode a dream scene.

REM is an activated sleep state with strong motor inhibition

The first REM period often appears roughly 70–120 minutes after sleep onset, and REM usually occupies more time in later cycles. Those are population patterns, not a clock that can reconstruct one person’s sleep stage from bedtime alone.

System map — open a layer

REM often shows low-voltage mixed-frequency scalp EEG and an activated brain state, but dreaming is not exclusive to REM. Modern EEG work also finds dream experience in NREM, with posterior cortical activity linked to dream reports.

Brainstem and medullary circuits strongly inhibit spinal motor output during REM, with glycinergic and GABAergic pathways contributing to atonia. Brief twitches, sleep paralysis, and dream enactment are different phenomena and should not be treated as one mechanism.

Deep dive: atonia lock →

PGO-like phasic activity is strongly established in animals and has limited direct human recordings. It is associated with REM cortical activity and may contribute to visual dream experience; direct scene-by-scene causality in humans remains unproven. Autonomic variability and reduced thermoregulatory control are better-established REM features.

REM dream mechanics

Below is the hard physiology of the process: cortex runs wake-like computation, the body is locked offline, and pontine spikes paint the visual field. Oneirox reads dreams against this machine — not against a symbol table.

REM is a neuroplasticity pass with the motors parked

Roughly ninety minutes after sleep onset the first REM episode opens (~10 minutes). Each later cycle stretches the window; near morning it can run 40–60 minutes. That elongation is when most vivid dream recall is harvested — and when Decode has the densest mechanism signal.

System map — open a layer

During REM, cortical EEG looks deceptively awake: high-frequency, low-amplitude beta/gamma chatter. The brain is consolidating, pruning unused synapses, and rewriting emotional traces — while external sensory intake is gated. That is why dream plots feel “real” even though the room is dark and silent.

Brainstem inhibitory drive floods the spinal cord. GABA and glycine clamp motoneurons so the dream script cannot be acted out. When this lock slips, you get twitches, sleep paralysis hangover, or partial REM behavior — exactly the somatic edges Oneirox utilities track.

Deep dive: atonia lock →

Ponto-geniculo-occipital spikes fire from brainstem into visual cortex, driving rapid eye movements and the hallucinated scene. Heart rate and breathing turn irregular; thermoregulation is blunted. The body is not “resting quietly” — it is running a chaotic internal projector with the heaters off.

PGO waves & autonomic chaos

PGO-like phasic activity is a real feature of REM physiology, demonstrated extensively in animals and recorded in limited human intracranial data. Its exact causal role in human dream imagery remains a research question, not a settled projector model.

What PGO evidence actually shows

Ponto-geniculo-occipital waves are well characterized in animals and PGO-like phasic potentials have been recorded in the human pons. They are associated with REM-related cortical activity, but it is too strong to say that they directly “paint” the visual content of every human dream.

Visual imagery: plausible contribution, not a caption generator

PGO activity has long been proposed as one contributor to internally generated visual experience in REM. That proposal is biologically plausible and supported by animal work, but the mapping from one PGO event to one dream image is not available in human dream reports.

Autonomic variability in REM

Heart rate, breathing, and thermoregulation can become more variable in REM, especially during phasic periods. Waking with a racing heart, irregular breath, heat, or chill can therefore be useful physiological context. It does not tell us what the dream “meant.”

How Oneirox should use this layer

Record autonomic residue as an observation: pulse, breath, sweating, temperature, eye-flutter memory. Then keep the inference modest. “Compatible with phasic REM/autonomic arousal” is scientifically defensible; “the body generated this exact dream plot” usually is not.

Human evidence reference: Lim et al., Sleep 2007, PMID 17682651. Review context: PGO waves remain an active but incomplete bridge between REM physiology and dream phenomenology.

Practical capture rule

Before you polish the plot: eye-flutter memory? irregular breath on wake? heat dump or chill? Those three map to PGO/autonomic load better than any dream-dictionary gloss.

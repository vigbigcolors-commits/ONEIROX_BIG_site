REM cortex & EEG

REM sleep often shows low-voltage, mixed-frequency scalp EEG, but dreaming is not exclusive to REM. Modern studies also find dream experience in NREM, with posterior cortical activity linked to whether and what people report dreaming.

What “wake-like” does and does not mean

Human REM sleep can look activated on scalp EEG compared with slow-wave sleep, but “the cortex is awake” is an oversimplification. Dreaming also occurs in NREM, and neural correlates of dream experience have been reported across both states. Sleep stage is context, not a dream decoder.

Dreaming, memory, and emotion

Sleep supports memory and affective regulation, and REM appears relevant to several emotional processes. What remains uncertain is whether the content of a particular dream reveals which memory or emotional operation was active. Dream-content studies are informative, but the evidence is mixed and method-dependent.

Do not infer pruning from a strange plot

Synaptic homeostasis and memory reorganization are active research areas in sleep science. A bizarre or compressed dream is not evidence that the brain was “pruning” a specific memory. Oneirox keeps those system-level theories separate from scene-level interpretation.

How to use this page

Use EEG and sleep-stage knowledge to narrow what is physiologically plausible. Then record dream content, waking body state, and timing as separate observations. A vivid dream can be biologically ordinary and still personally meaningful; the meaning is an interpretation, not an EEG result.

For a direct neural correlate of dream experience, see Siclari et al. 2017: dreaming in both REM and NREM was associated with local posterior cortical activity. That finding is stronger than the older shorthand that vivid dreaming is simply “REM cortex running like waking.”

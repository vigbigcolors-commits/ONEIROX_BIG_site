Dream About a Wedding Going Wrong

The dress doesn't fit, the venue is wrong, you're late, or the wrong person shows up at the altar. Wedding-disaster dreams are one of the most reliably misread dreams there is.

SIGNAL

A wedding is a high-visibility, high-stakes public transition — the dream is very often about the pressure and visibility of the event itself, not about doubts regarding the relationship.

What's actually happening

Surveys of engaged and recently married people find wedding-disaster dreams extremely common, and they don't correlate with actual relationship satisfaction or doubt in any consistent way. What they do correlate with is the sheer logistical and social pressure of the event: a wedding is one of the most public, most scrutinized, most planned single days most people ever have, and REM's threat-simulation machinery treats 'high-stakes public event' as fertile ground regardless of how solid the underlying relationship is.

This is a useful example of why a dream's surface content (your wedding) and its actual subject (visibility, performance pressure, the fear of something going wrong in front of witnesses) can be almost entirely separate things. The disaster in the dream is rarely a coded prediction; it's closer to your nervous system rehearsing 'what if this goes wrong' for an event you consciously know matters enormously.

Marriage dreams outside the wedding-day context — dreaming about getting married in general — tend to track a broader readiness or commitment theme, and follow patterns closer to the transition-anxiety seen in job-change or moving-house dreams than to relationship-specific content.

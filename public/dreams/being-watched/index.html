What's actually happening

Some dream content involves an identifiable threat: a chaser, an attacker, a specific danger. Being-watched dreams are different — they're defined by a diffuse sense of exposure or surveillance without a clear source. This pattern correlates with generalized hypervigilance: an alertness system that's activated but hasn't (or can't) localize a specific threat, which shows up in EEG as intrusions of faster, more wake-like activity into otherwise slower sleep stages — the brain staying partially 'on guard' even during sleep.

Being trapped or unable to escape a pressure you can't identify follows a similar logic, layered with the atonia-mismatch described elsewhere in this library: a felt sense of danger, combined with suppressed motor output, combined with no clear exit because the threat itself was never concrete enough to solve.

Both variants tend to correlate more with a general period of low psychological safety — feeling exposed, judged, or unable to control your circumstances — than with any single identifiable event or person.

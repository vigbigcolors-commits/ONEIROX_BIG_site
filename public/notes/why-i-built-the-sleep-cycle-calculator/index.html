Why I built the Sleep Cycle Calculator

Vigen G.R. 15 Aug 2026 · Oneirox Lab Notes

Decode is the reading you want when a dream is still in your chest. But most nights do not need a language model. They need a planning tool that respects how sleep is structured.

Human sleep moves in ultradian blocks that average around 90 minutes — NREM into REM, then again, four to six times a night. That rhythm is not a lifehack invented on the internet. It sits in the classic EEG work of Dement & Kleitman and in Kleitman’s broader rest–activity cycle. Cycle length is not a perfect clock — roughly 70–110 minutes, shifting across the night — so a good tool offers a window of options, not one rigid number.

Sleep inertia is not a personality flaw. It is what happens when an alarm cuts mid-cycle.

The problem I kept living

I kept waking with that thick, delayed clarity — reaction time off, mood flat, body still halfway in slow-wave or REM. The literature name is sleep inertia. It is strongest when you interrupt deep NREM. It is milder when you surface near a cycle boundary, in lighter sleep.

So I built a calculator that does only one job: take a wake time or a bedtime, add a realistic sleep-onset buffer (~14 minutes), and count in ~90-minute steps so you can land between cycles instead of inside one.

Why this belongs in the Lab, not behind Decode

This tool sends no data to a server. No API. No login. That is intentional. Oneirox is becoming a laboratory you run with your hands — Mapper, Phase, Cycles, somatic profiles — with Decode as the flagship reading when the dream itself needs a mechanism-based answer.

If you use Cycles to plan the night, and a dream still lingers in the morning, that is when you open Decode — optionally after the Sensory Dream Mapper.

Product note

The calculator is planning math, not diagnosis. Individual cycles vary. Use it as a boundary finder, not a medical schedule. Instrument: /lab/sleep-cycles/.
